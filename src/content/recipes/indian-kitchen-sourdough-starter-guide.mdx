instructions:
  - step: 1
    title: "Day 1 — Inoculation (Setting the Colony)"
    text: "In a clean 500ml wide-mouth glass jar, mix 50g Chakki Atta and 50ml chlorine-free water until no dry flour remains (thick paste consistency). Scrape down the jar walls cleanly with a spatula. Place a rubber band around the jar at the top level of the paste to monitor rise. Rest the lid loosely on top (do not screw tight—fermentation gas must vent freely) and leave in a warm kitchen spot (26°C–30°C) for 24 hours."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 2
    title: "Day 2 — First Micro-Bubbles"
    text: "You will notice tiny surface bubbles and a sweet, nutty wheat aroma. Stir well. Discard half (50g), leaving 50g in the jar. Feed with 50g Chakki Atta and 50ml chlorine-free water. Mix thoroughly until smooth, scrape down the jar sides, slide the rubber band to the new baseline, and rest loosely covered for 24 hours."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 3
    title: "Day 3 — The False Rise & Switch to 50:50 Blend"
    text: "Between Day 2 and Day 3, gas-producing Leuconostoc bacteria often cause a dramatic false rise followed by deflation. Discard down to 50g starter. From today onwards, feed the 50:50 Flour Blend: 25g Chakki Atta + 25g regular Maida (Fortune/Ganesh) + 50ml water. (Warm Kitchen Alert: If your kitchen is above 30°C and hooch liquid appears before 24 hours, feed every 12 hours)."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 4
    title: "Days 4 & 5 — Acidification & High-Ratio Feed"
    text: "As lactic acid bacteria lower the jar pH, true wild yeast (Saccharomyces exiguus) takes over. Discard down to 30g starter and switch to a high-ratio 1:2:2 feed: 30g Atta + 30g Maida + 60ml water. This larger meal prevents the starter from starving and turning vinegary in Indian ambient heat."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 5
    title: "Day 6 — The Float Test & Bake Readiness"
    text: "Your starter is mature when it doubles or triples in height within 4 to 5 hours of feeding at 28°C. It will show a domed top laced with airy webbing and smell like creamy sweet dahi and ripe fruit. Drop 1 tsp gently into a glass of room-temperature water: if it floats effortlessly on the surface, it is ready to bake country loaves!"
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 6
    title: "Long-Term Schedule A — Daily Counter Maintenance (Frequent Bakers)"
    text: "If baking 3 to 7 times weekly, keep the jar on your kitchen counter and feed once every 24 hours using a 1:4:4 ratio: retain just 10g starter scrapings, and feed 20g Atta + 20g Maida + 40ml cool water. (Indian Summer Tip: If ambient temp exceeds 32°C, reduce water to 32ml for an 80% stiff starter that ferments slower and resists sour acetone notes)."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 7
    title: "Long-Term Schedule B — Weekly Fridge Scrapings Method (Weekend Bakers)"
    text: "Zero-waste weekend routine: After mixing your weekend dough, leave just 15g–20g starter scrapings in the jar. Feed 20g Atta + 20g Maida + 35ml water (stiff 88% hydration). Leave on the counter for 45 minutes until active bubbling begins (15%–20% rise), then tighten the lid airtight and refrigerate at 4°C for up to 10 days with zero daily feeds. On Friday night before baking, take 20g starter, feed 20g Atta + 20g Maida + 40ml water (1:2:2), and leave on the counter overnight—it will peak by Saturday morning!"
    durationMinutes: 5
notes:
  - "Pure Flour & Water Only: Never add honey, jaggery, commercial yeast, or fruit juices on Day 1. Whole grain Chakki Atta naturally carries abundant wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria on its bran. Simple sugars like honey feed opportunistic early bacteria, delaying natural acidification."
  - "The RO Water Mineral Fix: Reverse Osmosis (RO) water lacks magnesium and calcium essential for wild yeast vigor, while municipal tap water contains chlorine that inhibits microbes. Use boiled-and-cooled tap water or add a micro-pinch (0.5g per litre) of Sendha Namak (rock salt) to RO water. Never use iodised salt."
  - "Indian Kitchen Heat Dynamics: At 28°C–34°C, fermentation moves twice as fast as European recipe books indicate. Track the volume increase using the rubber band rather than strict clock hours."
  - "Zero-Waste Philosophy: Never throw discard into the bin. Store collected discard in a separate jar in the fridge for up to 3 weeks to make instant sourdough jeera crackers, parathas, and cheelas."
