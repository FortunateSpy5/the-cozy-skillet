instructions:
  - step: 1
    title: "Cook the Tangzhong Gel"
    text: "In a small saucepan, whisk 20g maida and 100ml milk over medium-low flame until smooth and lump-free. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring continuously with a whisk or spatula. As soon as the mixture thickens into a glossy, pudding-like paste that leaves clear trails (reaching 65°C / 149°F), scrape into a bowl. Cover with plastic wrap or parchment touching the surface to prevent a skin from forming, and cool to room temperature (10 minutes in the fridge)."
    durationMinutes: 3
  - step: 2
    title: "Mix Liquids & Autolyse Rest"
    text: "In a large mixing bowl, combine lukewarm milk (110ml at 38°C / 100°F), honey, instant yeast, cooled Tangzhong paste, milk powder, and whisked dahi (if using). Add the maida (and optional atta/vital wheat gluten) and the salt. Stir with a wooden spoon or spatula until a shaggy, cohesive dough forms. Cover and rest undisturbed for 15 minutes. This autolyse window lets local maida starches fully hydrate, cutting physical kneading effort in half."
    durationMinutes: 15
  - step: 3
    title: "Knead in Softened Amul Butter"
    text: "Tip the rested dough onto an ungreased work surface. Smear half the softened (or cooled browned) Amul butter across the dough. Knead for 4 minutes using the heel of your hand or stretch-and-fold motion. Once absorbed, add the remaining butter and knead vigorously for another 5 to 6 minutes. The dough will transform from greasy to supple, silky, elastic, and pass the translucent windowpane test without tearing."
    durationMinutes: 10
  - step: 4
    title: "First Bulk Rise (Visual Doubling)"
    text: "Tuck edges underneath to shape dough into a smooth ball. Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover tightly with a damp tea towel or lid, and proof until doubled in size. In Indian summer (>30°C / 86°F), this takes approximately 45 minutes; in winter or air-conditioned kitchens (<22°C / 72°F), allow 75 minutes. Always judge by visual volume rather than strict clock time."
    durationMinutes: 45
    summerDurationMinutes: 45
    winterDurationMinutes: 75
  - step: 5
    title: "Shape for OTG Pav or Sandwich Loaf"
    text: "Gently press dough down to release large gas bubbles. For OTG Ladi Pav: Divide into 9 equal portions (~60g each). Cup your palm over each piece and roll firmly against the counter in circular motions until smooth and taut. Arrange in a greased 8x8 inch square aluminium baking tin in a 3x3 grid for classic bakery-style pull-apart pavs. For Sandwich Loaf: Roll dough into a 9x12-inch rectangle, fold into thirds like a letter, roll tightly into a log, pinch the seam, and place seam-side down in a greased 9x4-inch anodized aluminium loaf tin."
    durationMinutes: 8
  - step: 6
    title: "Second Proof & Poke Test"
    text: "Cover shaped dough loosely with a damp cloth and let rise until puffy and doubled (30 minutes in warm weather, 45 minutes in cool weather). Perform the poke test: press a flour-dusted fingertip 1/2-inch deep into the dough—it should spring back slowly halfway. Gently brush the top with milk and scatter sesame seeds if desired."
    durationMinutes: 30
    summerDurationMinutes: 30
    winterDurationMinutes: 45
  - step: 7
    title: "OTG Bake at 180°C & Foil-Tent Cue"
    text: "Preheat your OTG to 180°C (356°F) with Both Rods On (Top + Bottom, Fan Off) for a full 15 minutes. Place the baking tin on the lower-middle rack (one slot below center in 25L-35L OTGs; center rack in 40L+ OTGs). Bake for 22 minutes (for Ladi Pav) or 26-28 minutes (for Loaf). At the 15-minute mark, check top color: if already rich golden brown, lay a loose sheet of aluminium foil over the top for the remaining bake time to shield the dome from top heating rods. Immediately upon removal from the OTG, brush generously with 1 tbsp melted Amul butter and drape a clean damp cloth over the tin for 10 minutes to lock in steam."
    durationMinutes: 25
notes:
  - "Indian Flour Reality: Standard Indian Maida has 9.5-10.5% protein. The Tangzhong locks in moisture without waterlogging the dough. Adding 2% Vital Wheat Gluten (6g) turns standard Maida into commercial-grade bread flour."
  - "Salt Calibration: Amul Salted Butter contains ~2% salt. If using unsalted butter, increase fine salt from 5g to 6g."
