instructions:
  - step: 1
    title: "Knead the High-Hydration Dough"
    text: "In a mixing bowl, combine 220g maida, sugar, yeast, and salt. Pour in lukewarm water (180ml at 38°C (100°F)) and 1 tbsp olive oil. Stir with a wooden spoon or spatula until a sticky, shaggy dough forms. Turn onto an oiled surface and knead gently for 5 minutes with oiled hands until smooth, supple, and slightly tacky. Shape into a round ball."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 2
    title: "First Bulk Rise"
    text: "Place the dough ball into a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a damp kitchen towel or lid, and leave to proof in a warm draft-free spot until doubled in volume (45 minutes in warm kitchen, 75 minutes in cool kitchen)."
    durationMinutes: 60
    summerDurationMinutes: 45
    winterDurationMinutes: 75
  - step: 3
    title: "Blanch & Prep the Tomatoes"
    text: "Cut a shallow 'X' slit on the bottom of each tomato. Add the 6 tomatoes into a pot of rapidly boiling water, cover, and boil for 3 to 4 minutes until the skins split and curl away from the flesh. Lift out with tongs into cold water. Peel off and discard all skins. Take 3 tomatoes and blend to a smooth, velvety puree. Take the remaining 3 tomatoes, scoop out and discard seeds, and finely dice the firm pulp."
    durationMinutes: 4
  - step: 4
    title: "Sauté Aromatics & Build Sauce"
    text: "Heat 2 tsp olive oil in a non-stick pan or saucepan over medium flame. Add minced garlic and sauté until fragrant and lightly golden (do not let it burn). Add chopped onions and sauté for 2 minutes until translucent. Add the diced deseeded tomatoes and cook for 3 minutes, pressing them down with the back of a spoon or spatula until completely soft and mushy."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 5
    title: "Simmer & Emulsify Pomodoro"
    text: "Pour in the smooth tomato puree. Add chopped basil (or extra oregano), sugar, Kashmiri chilli powder, oregano, chilli flakes, and salt. Stir well and bring to a boil. Cover the pan and simmer on low flame for 5 minutes until the sauce turns thick and rich. Stir in 1 tbsp tomato sauce and 1/2 tbsp butter until melted and emulsified into a glossy sauce. Remove from heat."
    durationMinutes: 5
  - step: 6
    title: "Shape Dough in the Skillet"
    text: "Preheat your OTG to 230°C (446°F) with Both Rods On. Coat a seasoned 10-inch or 12-inch cast-iron skillet (or heavy tawa) with 1 tsp olive oil and dust with 1 tbsp fine sooji. Divide the proofed dough into 2 portions for thin-crust pan pizzas (or use the entire dough for a thick deep-dish skillet pizza). Gently stretch and dimple dough outward with fingertips, pushing a slightly raised outer rim (cornicione) against the skillet sides."
    durationMinutes: 3
  - step: 7
    title: "Stovetop Sear & Char (The Neapolitan Secret)"
    text: "Place the cast-iron skillet over medium-high flame on the stovetop for 2 to 3 minutes. Cook undisturbed until the bottom crust blisters, puffs up with steam, and turns golden-crisp with light charred spots underneath."
    durationMinutes: 3
  - step: 8
    title: "Top & Bake (OTG Finish)"
    text: "Remove skillet from the stovetop. Spread 4 to 5 tbsp of warm pomodoro sauce evenly across the dough, leaving a 1/2-inch border. Generously scatter shredded mozzarella and optional toppings. Transfer the skillet directly to the top rack of your preheated 230°C (446°F) OTG. Bake for 5 to 7 minutes until the cheese is bubbling with golden leopard spots and the crust rim is deeply bronzed. Brush the crust edge with garlic butter and serve hot."
    durationMinutes: 6
notes:
  - "Indian Kitchen Note on Tomatoes: Desi (country) tomatoes are naturally tarter and juicier, while hybrid (Roma/salad) tomatoes are meatier with lower water content. Using a 50/50 mix or adding 3/4 tsp sugar perfectly balances natural tartness."
  - "Cheese Selection: Amul Pizza Cheese (a blend of Mozzarella and Cheddar) is ideal for Indian kitchens; it provides a dramatic cheese pull while the cheddar adds a sharp, savory bite."
  - "Make-Ahead Storage: The scratch Pomodoro sauce keeps in an airtight glass jar in the refrigerator for up to 5 days, or can be frozen for up to 1 month. The dough can also be rested overnight in the fridge for enhanced fermentation flavor."
